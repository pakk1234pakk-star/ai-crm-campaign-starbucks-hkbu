# Final Reflection

This lab significantly improved my basic understanding of how Generative AI can support and improve the commercial campaign cycle — from ideation and customer journey design to live crisis recovery.

At the beginning, I treated AI mainly as a writing tool. After completing the three activities, I realised that AI is more useful when it is carefully controlled. The weak one-line prompt produced only generic ideas, while structured prompts (especially the CO-STAR framework) helped me guide the AI toward more practical and commercially relevant outputs. I learned that clear instructions about role, objective, constraints, and required format are essential if I want the AI to produce the work I actually need.

I also learned how to get effective help from AI “skills” or agents. Instead of typing long prompts every time, I can turn useful instructions into reusable skills. For example, the CX Crisis Triage skill allowed me to quickly transform messy social media and app feedback into a structured matrix. This made the process faster and more consistent. At the same time, the lab reminded me that AI still needs human checking — especially when extracting exact details such as “50 mins”, “35 minutes”, “$46”, and “6:45 PM” — to avoid hallucination.

Through the 7-day journey design and the Day 4 recovery plan, I further saw how AI can support different stages of the business cycle: generating ideas, designing automated workflows (IF/THEN logic), and proposing operational solutions under pressure. However, final decisions on priorities, margin protection, and customer experience still require managerial judgement.

Overall, this lab shifted my view of Generative AI from a writing assistant to a structured thinking partner for CRM strategy, operational recovery, and data-driven decision making. When used with clear frameworks, verification steps, and commercial constraints, it can meaningfully improve both the speed and quality of managerial work in real business settings.
